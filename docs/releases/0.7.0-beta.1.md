**Gridline 0.7.0 beta 1 is a beta.** It is published as a GitHub Pre-release, so Gridline's update check only offers it to people who turn on **Include beta versions** in **Settings, General, Updates**. Anyone can download it from this page, and 0.7.0 itself follows later. Projects from 0.6.1 open in this beta as they are: the project format has not changed. If something does not work as it should, please [open an issue on GitHub](https://github.com/lolitemaultes/Gridline/issues); every report helps get 0.7.0 ready.

The two big changes in 0.7.0 are **real-time collaboration**, where two or more people work on one project live, on the same network or across the internet with no server and no account, and a **refined interface**: panels and editors that pop out onto any monitor, every icon redrawn as an SVG, and plugin windows that give their editor more room. The beta also brings Plasma, a new wavetable synth that opens Serum presets as Plasma sounds, stand-ins for a missing Serum or Kickstart, one undo step per gesture in every plugin editor, Seltmann, the automatic sample chopper, and a Stop button that silences everything.

## Highlights

### Make music together, live

Open the Collaboration panel, give the project a name and press **Make collaborative**, then invite people with a one-time code that expires after 15 minutes to 24 hours. They paste it in **File, Join collaborative project...** and choose where their copy of the project goes. From then on channels, notes, the playlist, the mixer, automation, Gridline's plugin settings and audio files sync in a fraction of a second, and everyone keeps their own windows and transport.

- **Peer to peer, no server and no account.** Computers connect directly on the same network, through routers (hole punching and automatic port opening), through another collaborator, or through an optional TURN relay. **Test my connection** reports what your router allows.
- **Private by design.** Everything is end-to-end encrypted, every change is signed by its author, and safety codes let you check who you are talking to. Nothing goes anywhere but to the people you invited and the STUN or TURN server you configure.
- **See each other work.** Collaborators' cursors point at the same track and time, key, step, knob or mixer strip on every screen, whatever the window size, zoom or interface scale. They are drawn at the size of your own mouse pointer in each person's colour, and changes flash in their author's colour. People show in the top bar and in the panel with their profile picture, an online lamp and **Follow their playhead**.
- **Sound stays on your computer.** Playback, previews, held keys, solo and recording are never shared, and a collaborator's edits never make sound on yours.
- **Work offline.** The project saves itself continuously and stays a normal project folder. Changes travel the next time two computers that have them are online together, and edits made to the same thing while apart go to the admin's **Sync review**.
- **Undo takes back only your own changes**, plugin edits included.
- **Rename in place.** Click the project's name at the top of the Collaboration panel to rename it for everyone. **Recent collaborative projects** in the File menu reopens a session, and members reconnect by themselves.
- **People and files.** The admin can remove a member, and leaving or stopping sharing asks what happens to the project folder.

Collaborative projects use Gridline's own plugins only, and everyone needs the same Gridline version: for this beta, everyone in a session runs 0.7.0 beta 1. The full guide is in `docs/collaboration.md`.

### A refined interface

**Windows on every monitor.** Every panel (mixer, channel rack, playlist, piano roll, browser, ...) and every Gridline plugin editor has a pop-out button in its title bar: it becomes an ordinary window of your system that you can move to another monitor and resize by its edges, with its contents following the resize live. Its dock button or its own close button brings it back where it was. Shortcuts, the typing keyboard and drag and drop work in every window, and popped-out panels reopen where they were, on the same monitor. Plugin windows open inside the main window unless you pop them out. **View, Bring all windows back into Gridline** gathers everything, and **Settings, Interface, Allow windows outside the main window** turns the feature off.

**Every icon is an SVG.** The icons that were still drawn as shapes or text characters (menu and tree arrows, check marks, close crosses, the mixer and track header glyphs, playlist clip glyphs, plugin window header buttons, the audio editor's toolbar and plugin editor captions) now come from the same SVG set as the rest of the interface, with 88 new icons drawn for Gridline. Each one sits exactly where the old glyph was, so no layout moves. Tool icons in the playlist, piano roll and automation editor are one size each, the selected tool carries the same underline as the top bar's window buttons, play and pause match stop and record, and knob pointers are straight to their ends.

**More room for plugin editors.** The strip at the top of a plugin's channel window (volume, pan, pitch and the preview keyboard) is now hidden, and the eye button in the window's header slides it down when you need it. The window grows and shrinks with it, docked or popped out.

**Windows stay in reach.** Floating windows inside Gridline can no longer be dragged off the edge and lost: past an edge they follow with growing resistance and spring back when you let go (**Elastic edges** in **Settings, Interface**). The window layout now comes back at the next start, and the main window redraws continuously while you resize it.

**A clearer playlist.** Clicking a clip makes that clip, as it is, what the draw and paint tools place: a trimmed audio clip paints with its length, fades, gain, pitch and stretch. Where two audio clips crossfade, the overlap shows both blended the way they play.

### Plasma: a wavetable synth that opens Serum presets

Plasma (`gl.plasma`) is a new wavetable hybrid synth: three oscillators (wavetable, multisample, sample, granular or spectral) with two warp slots each, unison up to 16 voices, a sub oscillator, a noise layer and two filters. It has 4 envelopes, 10 LFOs, 8 macros and a 64-slot modulation matrix (drag a source onto any knob), three effect racks with up to 24 modules, an arpeggiator, a clip player and a wavetable editor.

Plasma opens Serum 2 presets (`.SerumPreset`) and Serum 1 `.fxp` files from your own folders and turns them into Plasma sounds, from the preset menu, the preset browser or by drag and drop. The browser's Serum library lists the Serum preset folders found on your computer, indexed in the background and searchable as a whole. Whatever was approximated is listed behind a small info mark next to the preset name. See `docs/plugins/plasma.md`.

### Stand-ins for a missing Serum or Kickstart

A project (Gridline or FL Studio) that uses Serum or Serum 2 on a computer where it is not installed now plays it with Plasma, and a missing Kickstart or Kickstart 2 plays with Tideline. The plugin's saved state is mapped onto the stand-in and its automation follows. The original plugin and its state stay in the project untouched, so once it is installed the project opens with it again. Installed plugins are always hosted as themselves. **Replace with Plasma** or **Replace with Tideline** in the plugin window keeps the stand-in for good.

### Undo in plugin editors, one step per gesture

Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z on macOS) and Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y now undo and redo what you did in any Gridline plugin editor, also in a popped-out window. Every gesture is one step: a knob drag, a turn of the mouse wheel, a typed value, a menu pick, one stroke on a drawn shape, an effect added or moved, a preset loaded. In external plugins (VST3, CLAP, AU, VST2), an edit in the plugin's own interface is one undo step too, and undo and redo no longer lose the redo history.

### Seltmann, the automatic sample chopper

Drop audio on Seltmann (`gl.seltmann`) and it works out what the sound is, its tempo and where bar 1 starts, chops it at a size that suits it, and plays as soon as you pick one of 59 layout cards. Breaks, melodic loops, whole songs, one-shots and textures all work. It plays as an instrument in time with your project, and loops drag out as audio files or patterns. See `docs/plugins/seltmann.md`.

### Stop silences everything

Stop now releases every sound, not only the song: notes from the piano roll and keyboards, a MIDI keyboard with its sustain pedal, pads, the arpeggiator, previews and installed plugins. Press Stop again to silence every tail at once with a short fade (also **Panic: stop all sound** in the Stop button's menu, Ctrl+H). Plugins' own Play buttons follow Stop, and Play with the transport stopped first stops a plugin that is playing on its own.

### Fixes

- The window layout (panel sizes, docking, the channel rack's position) was saved but never loaded; it now comes back at the next start.
- Resizing the main window on macOS and Windows no longer shows a stretched picture until the mouse is let go.
- Moving between Gridline's windows no longer releases held keys, and keys held when Gridline loses focus no longer get stuck.
- A wheel turn over a control is one undo step, not one step per notch, and two strokes on the same drawn shape no longer merge into one step.

The complete list of changes is in [`CHANGELOG.md`](https://github.com/lolitemaultes/Gridline/blob/v0.7.0-beta.1/CHANGELOG.md).

## Install notes

### macOS

1. Open the `.dmg` and drag **Gridline** onto **Applications**. The app is universal: it runs natively on Apple Silicon and Intel Macs.
2. The build is not notarized by Apple. The first time, right-click (or Control-click) Gridline in Applications, choose **Open**, then **Open** again. You can also allow it in **System Settings, Privacy & Security** with **Open Anyway**. If macOS says the app is damaged, run once in Terminal:
   ```sh
   xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Gridline.app
   ```
3. Plugins (optional): the `Plugins` folder in the disk image holds Gridline as a plugin for other hosts. Copy them to your user plugin folders and clear the quarantine flag the same way:
   - `Gridline.vst3` to `~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3`
   - `Gridline.clap` to `~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/CLAP`
   - `Gridline.component` to `~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components` (Audio Unit)
4. The command line tool `gridline` is in the disk image too; copy it somewhere on your `PATH`, such as `/usr/local/bin`.

### Windows

1. **Installer** (`-setup.exe`): installs Gridline in `Program Files` (it asks for administrator rights), with optional plugins (VST3 and CLAP into `Common Files`), project file types and a desktop icon. **Zip**: extract anywhere and run `gridline-app.exe`; keep the `assets` folder next to it.
2. The builds are not code signed. If SmartScreen shows "Windows protected your PC", choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
3. Plugins from the zip (optional): copy `plugins\VST3\Gridline.vst3` to `C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3`, and `plugins\CLAP\Gridline.clap` with `plugins\CLAP\Gridline-assets` to `C:\Program Files\Common Files\CLAP`.
4. Use the ARM64 files on Windows on ARM computers; the x64 files on everything else.

### Linux

- **AppImage:**
  ```sh
  chmod +x Gridline-0.7.0-beta.1-linux-x86_64.AppImage
  ./Gridline-0.7.0-beta.1-linux-x86_64.AppImage
  ```
- **Debian, Ubuntu:**
  ```sh
  sudo apt install ./Gridline-0.7.0-beta.1-linux-amd64.deb
  ```
- **Fedora, openSUSE:**
  ```sh
  sudo dnf install ./Gridline-0.7.0-beta.1-linux-x86_64.rpm      # or: sudo zypper install ./Gridline-0.7.0-beta.1-linux-x86_64.rpm
  ```
- **tar.gz:** extract anywhere (or into `/usr/local`) and run `bin/gridline-app`. It needs the system's SDL2 and ALSA libraries (JACK is optional).
- The packages install Gridline as a plugin in `/usr/lib/vst3` and `/usr/lib/clap`; from the tar.gz, copy `lib/vst3/Gridline.vst3` to `~/.vst3` and `lib/clap/Gridline.clap` to `~/.clap`.
- **Wayland:** Gridline runs natively through SDL. The interfaces of hosted third-party plugins are X11 windows and open through XWayland, which most desktops start on their own. Popping windows out of the main window needs X11 or XWayland (`SDL_VIDEODRIVER=x11`); with SDL's native Wayland driver everything stays inside.
- On aarch64 use the files with `aarch64` or `arm64` in their name.

### FreeBSD, OpenBSD, NetBSD

Extract the archive and run `bin/gridline-app` (the command line tool is `bin/gridline`). Install the libraries it needs first:

| System | Packages |
|---|---|
| FreeBSD | `pkg install sdl2 sndio libX11 mesa-libs` |
| OpenBSD | `pkg_add sdl2` (X11, Mesa and sndio are part of the base system) |
| NetBSD | `pkgin install SDL2 sndio` (with the X11 sets installed) |

Audio goes through SDL (sndio or OSS), MIDI devices through sndio, and plugin interfaces open on X11.

## FL Studio project compatibility

Gridline reads and writes FL Studio project files so existing work can move in both directions:

- **Import:** all 164 demo projects that ship with FL Studio import without errors and with an exact structure (channels, patterns, notes, playlist, automation, markers, mixer routing and plugin settings), and play back with their sound.
- **Export:** projects saved as `.flp` or as an FL Studio zipped loop package open in FL Studio 2026. Unchanged parts of an imported project are written back exactly as they were read.
- **Gridline-only plugins** are written as stand-ins so the exported song still sounds right: close FL Studio equivalents where one exists, otherwise the part is rendered to audio and played by a sampler.
- Third-party plugins load as the installed plugin with their saved state, or stay as placeholders until it is installed. A missing Serum, Serum 2, Kickstart or Kickstart 2 plays through its Gridline stand-in, and exporting writes the original plugin back.

Gridline is an independent project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Image-Line. "FL Studio" is a trademark of Image-Line Software and is mentioned only to describe file compatibility. Serum and Kickstart are named only to describe preset and project compatibility; Gridline is not affiliated with their makers.

## Known limitations

- **This is a beta.** Collaboration in particular is new: please keep a copy of projects that matter, and tell us in an issue what went wrong.
- **Collaboration** works with Gridline's own plugins only (projects with VST3, VST2, CLAP, AU or LV2 plugins cannot become collaborative), and everyone in a session needs the same Gridline version.
- **Unsigned builds:** the macOS app is not notarized and the Windows files are not code signed, so the first start needs the steps above.
- **Plugin editors outside macOS:** the native interfaces of hosted plugins on Windows, Linux and the BSDs are built and tested automatically, but have not yet been tried by hand.
- **Sandboxed plugins on Windows** play and save normally, but their editor window is a placeholder for now; open the plugin outside the sandbox to see its interface.
- **Windows on ARM64** is built on every change but its tests do not run in CI yet.
- **Wayland:** plugin interfaces open through XWayland, not as native Wayland windows, and popping windows out needs XWayland.
- **Halftone and Alloy:** about 75 advanced settings (some filters, strum, reverb and delay options) are listed but not built yet; they are marked as such and do nothing for now.
- **Sample Keys** plays FL Studio's piano samples from an installed copy of FL Studio and is silent without it. Gridline's own piano is planned.

## Checksums

`Gridline-0.7.0-beta.1-SHA256SUMS` lists the SHA-256 checksum of every file. Put it in the same folder as your download and run:

```sh
# Linux, BSD
sha256sum --ignore-missing -c Gridline-0.7.0-beta.1-SHA256SUMS
# macOS
shasum -a 256 --ignore-missing -c Gridline-0.7.0-beta.1-SHA256SUMS
```

On Windows (PowerShell), compare the output with the line for your file:

```powershell
Get-FileHash .\Gridline-0.7.0-beta.1-windows-x64-setup.exe -Algorithm SHA256
```

On FreeBSD and OpenBSD, `sha256 Gridline-0.7.0-beta.1-freebsd-x86_64.tar.gz` prints the checksum to compare.
